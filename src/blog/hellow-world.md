I enjoy writing, and have always enjoyed it. I have the proud distinction of having created a handful of websites starting when I was eight, and I actively contributed to the fragmented blogosphere for around seven or eight years, from Tumblr to Weebly to (briefly) Wix and Wordpress.

Of course, I do not remember most of those URLs and the ones that I do will remain in a mostly lost corner of the internet under whatever pseudonym I had chosen that day so long as I can help it. 

In the meantime, life got busy, and I became more aware. Paying attention to the world around you has a way of making silence easy; there's far too much of everything to pay attention to. 

Part of the reason for that nowadays is inescapably AI. It's safe to assume a lot of what you read day-to-day has gone through a large language model. This is also true of emails and Slack messages. And lots of people can generate large amounts of content. I mourn the days when longer messages meant that a person had taken time to write it. I used to spend time crafting what I wanted to say in the proper order or format to explain an issue, receive help, or persuade. 

I also mourn the loss of the em dash. I discovered it in high school when I would spend hours writing essays by hand, and I found they helped connect my thoughts. I even started using em dashes in my texts. 

---

I learned to type when I was 9, and having been a software engineer for several years, I have learned that my hands when typing `hello world` feel the urge to write `hellow world` with an extra `w` anticipating the next word. 

I do wonder how much time I've spent over my life backspacing and fixing typos. I suppose AI helps get rid of such insignificant behavior. Although, it gives me a feeling of realness when I get to edit my own thoughts. 

Yet at the same time, I am also guilty of purposely NOT fixing such typos nowadays so that readers may know I was typing manually, and therefore was earnestly communicating. 

---

I will not pretend I don't use AI to write or edit. AI has changed the way I work and think, and that does not inherently devalue content. Instead, it is important to be honest, and somewhere in the past several months, I learned about [this project to annotate AI attributions](https://aiattribution.github.io/) that I will use. 

The point of this space, however, is to reflect. I think the practice I had when I was younger to write when I felt like it and to have a personal space on the internet was fulfilling. And by doing this now, I hope I will regain that feeling.
